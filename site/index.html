Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement

Original abstract

The introduction of generalist robot policies has enabled a single, large model to learn to perform a variety of tasks from a vast pre-training corpus. While these models often exhibit powerful zero-shot generalization capabilities, they typically require an adaptation phase to achieve sufficient performance in deployment. Iterated self-improvement via reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated promise in enabling this adaptation, yet such approaches have been largely explored in single-task settings - where the goal is to simply improve on a narrow deployment task - which lies in stark contrast to the multi-task capabilities of the base policy. In this work we ask whether generalist robot policies are capable of multi-task, generalist improvement in deployment - targeting many diverse tasks simultaneously - and what algorithmic recipe enables this. To answer this question, we first develop a set of structured evaluations to test generalist improvement capabilities, and systematically evaluate algorithmic and practical design choices regarding value estimation, policy extraction and resource allocation. We show that not only can generalist policies be improved online across diverse tasks, but that the right recipe can enable generalization and increase performance on tasks not considered during online training. We characterize when this generalization occurs and show that both the training task distribution and the use of value-based RL are critical. To the best of our knowledge, this is among the first evidence for online generalist improvement for robot foundation models.

Which Physical Tactile Cues Matter for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning? A Controlled Real-Robot Study

Xuhao Qin, Xiyan Huang, Haoyu Dong, Xi Zhang, Chenxi Xiao

Published 2026-11-04 23:45 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO

Takeaway

Vision-based tactile sensors encode contact appearance, geometry, indentation, and tangential deformation in one image stream, but policies that process tactile images directly obscure which physical cues drive learning. This controlled real-robot study on the LeRobot platform derives tactile RGB images, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields from the same stream and compares their individual and combined contributions under identical demonstrations and policy backbone, also comparing tactile encoders and fusion strategies. Across 720 real-robot trials, tactile perception raises overall success from 57.8% to 78.9%, marker flow is the most effective single cue at 74.4%, cue-factorized encoding consistently helps, and cross-attention fusion is comparable to pretrained tactile encoders. Inspect how cue-factorized encoding is implemented and how task-stage dependence is measured.

Original abstract

Vision-based tactile sensors capture multiple aspects of physical interaction within a single image stream, including contact appearance, geometry, indentation depth, and tangential deformation. However, existing visuo-tactile policies often directly process tactile observations as images, making it difficult to determine which physical cues contribute to policy learning. This paper presents a controlled real-robot study that compares physically interpretable tactile signals using the LeRobot platform, enhanced with our visuo-tactile perception system. From the same tactile stream, we derive tactile RGB images, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields, representing contact appearance, geometry, and tangential deformation, respectively. Using identical task demonstrations and the same policy backbone, we evaluate the individual and combined contributions of these tactile cues. We further compare tactile encoders and visuo-tactile fusion strategies. Evaluation across 720 real-robot trials yields four key findings: (1) tactile perception substantially improves the overall success rate from 57.8% to 78.9%, although its contribution varies across task stages; (2) marker flow is the most effective individual cue, achieving a success rate of 74.4% and playing a particularly important role in regulating established contact; (3) cue-factorized encoding consistently improves performance, highlighting that different physical cues require tailored processing techniques; and (4) although the effectiveness of tactile fusion is task-dependent, cross-attention offers a lightweight solution with performance comparable to that of pretrained tactile encoders.

H^2MARL: Hierarchical Heterogeneous Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot Control

Xuemin Hu, Kaixin Li, Qingqing Xia, Yuxin Wang, Wei Xie, Bo Tang, Long Chen

Published 2026-11-04 21:48 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO Reading focus humanoid_whole_body, whole_body_control

Takeaway

Humanoid whole-body control is difficult because of high-dimensional coupled dynamics and physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom, and existing multi-agent RL methods use coarse partitions or optimize planning and execution separately. H^2MARL formulates control with a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, conditioning each torque-actuated JDoF on a descriptor of its actuation, kinematic, and dynamic properties, and adds a closed-loop policy-executor co-optimization mechanism that jointly adapts the high-level pose planner and low-level joint executor using shared interaction trajectories. Experiments in MuJoCo and Isaac Gym report consistent improvements in locomotion stability, gait coordination, and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained, and independently optimized alternatives. Inspect how the JDoF descriptors are constructed and how co-optimization reduces planner-executor distribution mismatch.

Original abstract

Coordinated whole-body control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to high-dimensional coupled dynamics and substantial physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom (JDoFs). Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods either employ coarse agent partitions or directly apply generic heterogeneous-agent algorithms, while hierarchical controllers commonly optimize high-level planning and low-level execution independently, leading to uncoordinated motion. In this paper, we propose H^2MARL, a hierarchical heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with two key mechanisms. We first formulate humanoid whole-body control using a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, where each torque-actuated JDoF is conditioned on an explicit descriptor of its actuation, kinematic, and dynamic properties. Moreover, we develop a closed-loop policy-executor co-optimization mechanism that jointly adapts the high-level pose planner and the low-level joint executor using shared interaction trajectories, thereby reducing the distribution mismatch between planning and execution. Extensive experiments in MuJoCo and Isaac Gym demonstrate that H^2MARL consistently improves locomotion stability, gait coordination, and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained, and independently optimized alternatives.

Robocasa-Mem: Benchmarking Environment and Process Memory in Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation

Sihang Chen, Chen Ye, Wei Chen, Xiaohan Lei, Zhipeng Tang, Sha Zhang, Yuan Zhang, Zixuan Chen, Jiajun Deng, Houqiang Li, Yanyong Zhang

Original abstract

In the real world, mobile manipulation robots must perform complex object interactions and make multi-step decisions, often relying on task-relevant information that is no longer observable. However, existing benchmarks provide limited evaluation of such memory-dependent decision-making in mobile manipulation. To address this gap, we introduce RoboCasa-Mem, a new benchmark comprising nine mobile manipulation tasks that require agents to use interaction history at key decision points. Specifically, the tasks are grouped into two categories of memory: One is environment memory, which captures spatial locations, object attributes, and object relationships. The other is process memory, which captures single-task progress and multi-task scheduling. Particularly, we introduce Behavior-Conditioned Evaluation. For tasks with multiple valid execution paths, instead of fixing a single target or trajectory, the evaluator derives later task constraints from the robot's earlier actions. This allows us to evaluate whether later behavior is consistent with the robot's own interaction history while reducing shortcuts based on fixed target assignments. We provide 1,800 successful demonstrations for policy training. We also develop a baseline on our benchmark, namely Hierarchical Memory-Augmented Policy(HMAP). Its high-level planner uses structured entity and process memories to generate subtasks. Its low-level VLA controller uses subgoal-conditioned queries to incorporate VGGT-Omega geometry into action generation. Together, RoboCasa-Mem and HMAP baseline support systematic evaluation of memory-dependent behavior and the development of memory-aware mobile manipulation policies.
