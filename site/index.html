Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations

Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov

Published 2026-11-03 02:59 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG, eess.SY

Pixel-UMI addresses the embodiment gap when learning whole-body mobile manipulation policies from sensorized human demonstrations. Environment-Aware Retargeting combines reconstructed scene geometry with demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to produce collision-aware joint and base targets, delay-calibrated action assignment converts them into actions, Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking downweights mismatched viewpoints during training, and on-policy human corrections via DAgger target contact-rich interactions. Original abstract

Sensorized human demonstrations offer scalable data for mobile manipulation, but embodiment differences complicate policy learning. We present Pixel-UMI, a pipeline for learning environment-aware whole-body policies from human data. We first introduce Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR), which combines reconstructed scene geometry with demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to determine robot posture and generate collision-aware joint and base targets. Delay-calibrated action assignment converts these trajectories into actions. However, kinematic differences can leave residual pose errors, creating viewpoint mismatches between human demonstrations and robot observations. We therefore introduce Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM), which uses these errors during training to reduce reliance on mismatched views at deployment. Finally, because estimated actions cannot fully capture contact-rich interactions, we collect on-policy human corrections through DAgger to improve performance. We evaluate Pixel-UMI on five challenging tasks spanning articulated-object manipulation, deformable-object handling, constrained-space reaching, whole-body collision avoidance, and contact-rich bimanual coordination, achieving 87.0% average task progress. Video results are available at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel-umi

Original abstract

Fine-grained manipulation couples instruction grounding, local visual precision, and sustained execution. We present FineCraft, a framework that adapts pretrained models for fine-grained manipulation in the MetaFine Challenge. A task-routed mixture of policies selects independently adapted DINOv2-DiT or pretrained $π_{0.5}$ policies through a common observation-action interface. Local visual features help identify the instructed object parts, demonstrations and expert continuations teach the required actions, and new observations guide replanning during execution. FineCraft ranks fourth with an overall score of $0.878$, exceeding the published reference on all five tasks. Official results and local diagnostics characterize performance across visual perturbations, instruction changes, and multi-stage manipulation. Code is available at https://github.com/eqcy/FineCraft

Anti-Gravity Walking by a Flying Humanoid Robot via Thrust-Rate Input Whole-Body Model Predictive Control

Kazuki Sugihara, Kei Okada

Published 2026-09-18 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation LettersReading focus model_predictive_control, trajectory_optimization

Takeaway

This work formulates real-time whole-body MPC for anti-gravity bipedal walking by a flying humanoid, where contact switching during walking causes discontinuous dynamic structure and thrust spikes. Using thrust-rate as the control input keeps thrust trajectories continuous across contact switches while preserving the sparse optimal-control structure for fast computation, and lower bounds on the foot-normal contact force are introduced and smoothly transferred during double support. The authors report simulation and hardware demonstration of anti-gravity walking, describing it as the first demonstration of multi-contact whole-body MPC for a transformable aerial robot and walking beyond the ground. Inspect the thrust-rate formulation and contact-force bound transfer.

Original abstract

Flying humanoids are expected to perform tasks in diverse environments, while their existing locomotion is mainly limited to aerial flight and ground walking. The capability to move in complex three-dimensional space can greatly expand their application range. For such walking motion on ceilings and similar anti-gravity environments, whole-body MPC is effective. However, the discontinuous changes in dynamic structure accompanying contact switching during walking can induce thrust spikes, resulting in control instability. Therefore, in this work, we propose and implement a real-time whole-body MPC framework for anti-gravity bipedal walking. First, we formulate whole-body MPC using the time derivative of thrust, namely thrust-rate, as the control input. This formulation guarantees continuity of the thrust trajectory during contact switching while preserving the sparse structure of the optimal control problem for fast computation. Second, we address the lack of natural support forces in anti-gravity environments. We introduce lower bounds on the foot-normal component of the contact force, and smoothly transfer them during the doublesupport phase. Finally, we implement the proposed framework and demonstrate anti-gravity walking by a flying humanoid through simulation and a hardware experiment. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first demonstration of multi-contact whole-body MPC for a transformable aerial robot and walking by a flying humanoid beyond the ground.

A Survey on Vision--Language--Action Models for Embodied AI

Yueen Ma, Zixing Song, Yuzheng Zhuang, Jianye Hao, Irwin King

Published 2026-04-28 Source IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems

Takeaway

It also summarizes relevant resources including datasets, simulators, and benchmarks, then discusses challenges and future directions, with a curated repository linked. Use it as a knowledge map for locating work along the component, policy, and planner axes and for finding associated datasets and benchmarks; it is a review and does not propose a new method.

Original abstract

Embodied AI is widely recognized as a cornerstone of artificial general intelligence (AGI) because it involves controlling embodied agents to perform tasks in the physical world. Building on the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), a new category of multimodal models-referred to as vision-language-action (VLA) models-has emerged to address language-conditioned robotic tasks in embodied AI by leveraging their distinct ability to generate actions. The recent proliferation of VLAs necessitates a comprehensive survey to capture the rapidly evolving landscape. To this end, we present the first survey on VLAs for embodied AI. This work provides a detailed taxonomy of VLAs, organized into three major lines of research. The first line focuses on individual components of VLAs. The second line is dedicated to developing VLA-based control policies adept at predicting low-level actions. The third line comprises high-level task planners capable of decomposing long-horizon tasks into a sequence of subtasks, thereby guiding VLAs to follow more general user instructions. Furthermore, we provide an extensive summary of relevant resources, including datasets, simulators, and benchmarks. Finally, we discuss the challenges facing VLAs and outline promising future directions in embodied AI. A curated repository associated with this survey is available at: https://github.com/yueen-ma/Awesome-VLA.
