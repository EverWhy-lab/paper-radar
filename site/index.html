RoboEvo$^2$: Recursive Robot Self-Improvement via Harness-Policy Co-Evolution

Chenzhi Liu, Jiehong Lin, Wei Huang, Xuzhao Huang, Haoyuan Li, Yang Zhang, Junchao Huang, Chenyeyang Cai, Bo Wang, Kazuhiro Kosuge, Zhongrui Wang, Xiaojuan Qi

Original abstract

Robots should adapt to unfamiliar environments and acquire new skills through interaction, then execute those skills efficiently with practice. Vision-language-action policies provide efficient control, but often need additional human demonstrations to handle unfamiliar settings; reasoning agents can discover solutions using broad prior knowledge and robot-control APIs, but repeated deliberation and repair are costly. Inspired by human learning through reasoning and practice, we propose RoboEvo$^2$, a framework for recursive harness-policy co-evolution. In Policy-to-Harness (P2H) evolution, policy failures guide a coding agent to search complementary Code, Code+ICL, and Code+Policy routes, consolidating verified solutions into reusable skills, routing strategies, and experience memory. In Harness-to-Policy (H2P) evolution, verified trajectories provide supervised training, while Counterfactual Preference Distillation (CPD) teaches the policy to favor successful corrections over failures from the same starting state. The improved policy provides stronger motor skills for subsequent search, while its remaining failures redirect harness evolution, closing the reciprocal loop. Through recursive co-evolution without additional human demonstrations, RoboEvo$^2$ improves standalone success from 11.0% to 84.1% and hybrid success from 30.4% to 90.5% on LIBERO-PRO, with similar gains and reduced agentic costs on RoboTwin C2R. Further experiments demonstrate autonomous task acquisition, cross-embodiment harness transfer, and 95.0% hybrid success on the Unitree G1-D. Our models and code will be publicly released.

DemoFoundry: Toward Scalable Photorealistic Robot Data with Coding Agents and Generative Rendering

Ruixiang Wang, Xinyue Yao, Xuanning Zhou, Yueci Deng, Yongyi Su, Kui Jia

Published 2026-11-26 00:37 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO, cs.AI

Takeaway

DemoFoundry addresses the tension in scaling robot learning between simulation's visual gap and video-only generation's weak physical consistency and missing action labels, presenting an automated framework that couples coding agents with generative rendering. Coding agents build aligned real-proxy video pairs from existing robot episodes to train a video renderer and write executable interaction programs; given a scene reference and task instruction, an agent reconstructs task-relevant geometry and writes a control program without target-task human demonstrations, and program execution in a physics simulator yields proxy videos with aligned robot states and actions that the renderer converts into photorealistic observations. Automated task-execution checks select successful trajectories for rendering and policy learning, and experiments report improved visual quality and action-video consistency, with policies trained on generated demonstrations outperforming raw-simulation training and approaching equal numbers of real demonstrations. Inspect the execution checks and the real-proxy pairing.

Original abstract

Scaling robot learning requires demonstrations that combine realistic visual observations with reliable action supervision. Simulation-based synthesis faces a substantial visual gap relative to real environments, while video-only generation struggles to maintain physically consistent interactions and provide aligned action labels. We present DemoFoundry, an automated framework that couples coding agents with generative rendering to synthesize photorealistic and physically grounded robot demonstrations. Coding agents both construct aligned real--proxy video pairs from existing robot episodes to train a video renderer and write executable interaction programs to generate new demonstrations. Given a scene reference and task instruction, an agent reconstructs task-relevant geometry and writes a control program without requiring target-task human demonstrations. Program execution in a physics simulator produces proxy videos with aligned robot states and actions. The video renderer translates these proxy videos into scene-consistent, photorealistic observations, reducing the need for detailed appearance modeling in each scene. Automated task-execution checks select successful trajectories for rendering and policy learning. Experiments show improved visual quality and action--video consistency. Policies trained on the generated demonstrations outperform raw-simulation training and approach the performance of training on an equal number of real demonstrations.

Compliant residual DAgger: Improving real-world contact-rich manipulation with human corrections

Xiaomeng Xu, Yifan Hou, Chendong Xin, Zeyi Liu, Shuran Song

Published 2026-11-02 Source The International Journal of Robotics Research

Takeaway

CR-DAgger addresses two challenges in Dataset Aggregation for real-world contact-rich manipulation: collecting informative human correction data and effectively updating policies with it. It introduces a Compliant Intervention Interface that uses compliance control so humans can give gentle, accurate delta action corrections without interrupting ongoing policy execution, plus a Compliant Residual Policy formulation that learns from corrections while incorporating force feedback and force control. Original abstract

We address key challenges in Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) for real-world contact-rich manipulation: how to collect informative human correction data and how to effectively update policies with this new data. We introduce Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger), which contains two novel components: (1) a Compliant Intervention Interface that leverages compliance control, allowing humans to provide gentle, accurate delta action corrections without interrupting the ongoing robot policy execution; and (2) a Compliant Residual Policy formulation that learns from human corrections while incorporating force feedback and force control. Our system significantly enhances performance on precise contact-rich manipulation tasks using minimal correction data, improving base policy success rates by 64% on four challenging tasks (book flipping, belt assembly, cable routing, and gear insertion) while outperforming both retraining-from-scratch and finetuning approaches. Through extensive real-world experiments, we provide practical guidance for implementing effective DAgger in real-world robot learning tasks. Result videos are available at: https://compliant-residual-dagger.github.io/ .

Nav4Manip: From Navigation to Dexterous Mobile Manipulation through Co-Evolution

Yusen Feng, Zixi Kang, Zhijun Yin, Changxiao Liu, Tianhong Gao, Jiazhao Zhang, Jiahang Liu, Libin Liu, Zhizheng Zhang, He Wang

Original abstract

Dexterous mobile manipulation is essential for robots to perform useful tasks in complex, unstructured environments. However, existing systems face two challenges. First, navigation remains too coarse for downstream manipulation, which often requires a precise base position and approach orientation rather than merely reaching a meter-scale goal region. Second, navigation and manipulation are commonly trained in isolation, lacking a feedback loop that enables the two capabilities to co-evolve. These limitations prevent current systems from reliably executing long-horizon dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks in real-world environments. To address them, we introduce Nav4Manip, a bidirectionally coupled co-evolution system for long-horizon dexterous mobile manipulation. It comprises two complementary architectures: Nav4Manip-N learns long-horizon navigation and refines its policy through manipulation feedback, while Nav4Manip-M trains a world-action model (WAM) for downstream dexterous manipulation. We train Nav4Manip-N in three stages: multi-task co-tuning learns navigation behavior and manipulation-aware affordance prediction; rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) rejects low-quality policy-generated paths and fine-tunes the model on retained trajectories; and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) uses counterfactual preferences to further improve navigation decisions. To support this framework, we establish the Nav-N-Manip benchmark and construct Nav4Manip-Pairs from benchmark rollouts, providing large-scale paired navigation--manipulation trajectories and affordance supervision. Extensive experiments on public vision-language navigation (VLN) benchmarks and Nav-N-Manip demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, while real-world evaluations show that Nav4Manip completes diverse dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks. Project website: https://nav4manip.github.io/.

Design and Control of a Spherical Dexterous Robotic Eye for Humanoid Robot Applications

Chenjie Liu, Zhijian Xue, Kun Bai

Published 2026-10-22 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters

Takeaway

This paper presents a direct-drive spherical robotic eye (SRE) for humanoid active vision, in which torque is generated directly through interaction between stator coils and rotor permanent magnets, enabling continuous two-degree-of-freedom motion without mechanical transmission mechanisms. The authors develop kinematic and dynamic models, magnetic-field-based orientation sensing, and a cascaded control framework for eye motion, image-based target tracking, and line-of-sight (LOS) stabilization. Experiments report closed-loop bandwidths exceeding 48 Hz in both axes and a peak angular velocity of 2400deg/s, and on a Unitree G1 humanoid the system maintains target locking under dynamic robot-target motion and suppresses walking-induced image jitter through compensatory eye motion. Inspect the sensing and cascaded control design behind the reported bandwidth and stabilization.

Original abstract

This paper presents a direct-drive spherical robotic eye for humanoid active vision. The proposed spherical robotic eye (SRE) directly generates torque through the interaction between stator coils and rotor permanent magnets, enabling continuous two-degree-of-freedom motion without mechanical transmission mechanisms. Kinematic and dynamic models, magnetic-field-based orientation sensing, and a cascaded control framework are developed for eye motion, image-based target tracking, and line-of-sight (LOS) stabilization. Experiments show that the SRE achieves closed-loop bandwidths exceeding 48 Hz in both axes and a peak angular velocity of 2400deg/s. Integrated on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the system maintains target locking under dynamic robot–target motion and suppresses walking-induced image jitter through compensatory eye motion. The proposed SRE provides a compact and high-bandwidth active-vision module for humanoid robots.
