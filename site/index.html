Rewind-IL: Online Failure Detection and State Respawning for Imitation Learning

Gehan Zheng, Sanjay Seenivasan, Matthew Johnson‐Roberson, Weiming Zhi

Published 2026-09-16 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters

Takeaway

Rewind-IL targets deployment failures in long-horizon action-chunked imitation policies that drift off the demonstration manifold and keep producing plausible but unrecovered actions. It is a training-free online safeguard combining a zero-shot failure detector, the Temporal Inter-chunk Discrepancy Estimate (TIDE), calibrated with split conformal prediction, and a state-respawning mechanism that rewinds to a semantically verified safe intermediate state; a vision-language model selects recovery checkpoints offline, and the frozen policy encoder builds a checkpoint feature database. Real-world and simulated long-horizon manipulation experiments, including transfer to flow-matching action-chunked policies, are reported without numbers. Original abstract

Imitation learning has enabled robots to acquire complex visuomotor manipulation skills from demonstrations, but deployment failures remain a major obstacle, especially for long-horizon action-chunked policies. Once execution drifts off the demonstration manifold, these policies often continue producing locally plausible actions without recovering from the failure. Existing runtime monitors either require failure data, over-trigger under benign feature drift, or stop at failure detection without providing a recovery mechanism. We present Rewind-IL, a training-free online safeguard framework for generative action-chunked imitation policies. Rewind-IL combines a zero-shot failure detector based on Temporal Inter-chunk Discrepancy Estimate (TIDE), calibrated with split conformal prediction, with a state-respawning mechanism that returns the robot to a semantically verified safe intermediate state. Offline, a vision-language model identifies recovery checkpoints in demonstrations, and the frozen policy encoder is used to construct a compact checkpoint feature database. Online, Rewind-IL monitors self-consistency in overlapping action chunks, tracks similarity to the checkpoint library, and, upon failure, rewinds execution to the latest verified safe state before restarting inference from a clean policy state. Experiments on real-world and simulated long-horizon manipulation tasks, including transfer to flow-matching action-chunked policies, demonstrate that policy-internal consistency coupled with semantically grounded respawning offers a practical route to improved reliability in imitation learning. Supplemental materials are available at https://sjay05.github.io/rewind-il

Energy-efficient precision weed elimination using a quadruped robot

Shuangyu Xie, Di Wang, Joe Johnson, Chengsong Hu, Kaiyuan Chen, Muthukumar Bagavathiannan, Ken Y. Goldberg, Dezhen Song

Published 2026-10-18 Source The International Journal of Robotics Research

Takeaway

This work formalizes Repetitive and Clustered-Coupled Active Perception and Manipulation (RC-CAPM), an energy-efficient long-horizon planning problem for mobile manipulators in precision weeding that combines ordered, repetitive, heterogeneous tasks under observe-first-and-manipulate-later temporal constraints and reachability constraints. Simulation and physical mobile-manipulator weeding evaluations report an average 36.1% energy reduction over the baseline, with reductions in path length, stops, and replanning events. Original abstract

In precision agriculture, an autonomous weed-removal robot (i.e., mobile manipulator) often faces a combination of challenges including large-scale navigation, repetitive coverage, and fine-grained intervention. These challenges give rise to a distinct energy-efficient long-horizon planning problem: coordinating ordered, repetitive, and heterogeneous tasks under domain-specific temporal and geometric constraints. In precision weeding, this problem is further characterized by (i) an observe-first-and-manipulate-later type temporal constraint and (ii) the potential for substantial energy savings through efficient task collocation. We formalize this challenge and propose a new problem: Repetitive and Clustered-Coupled Active Perception and Manipulation (RC-CAPM) problem for mobile manipulators. We model RC-CAPM as a stochastic renewal process and show, via the Renewal Reward Theorem, that the expected energy consumption per task cycle converges to a well-defined long-run average. We introduce a task-space partitioning method that enumerates all feasible task-multiplexing configurations and their associated probabilities under robot reachability constraints. We then derive a two-stage optimization framework and prove that its solution is optimal and equivalent to that of the original RC-CAPM problem. We evaluate the proposed approach in both simulation and on a physical mobile manipulator performing autonomous weeding. Across all evaluations, RC-CAPM consistently outperforms baseline planning strategies, yielding reductions in path length, number of stops, replanning events, and total energy consumption. In particular, the proposed method achieves an average energy reduction of 36.1% relative to the baseline.
