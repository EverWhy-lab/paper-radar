Single-Controller Teleoperation and Multimodal Dataset Generation for a Mobile Manipulator

Fardien Azizi, Sitor Sla, Christiaan Peppelman, Dion van Santen, Andrei-Carlo Papuc, Khaled Mustafa, Diego Martinez-Baselga, Javier Alonso-Mora

Original abstract

Robot learning systems require synchronized, high-quality demonstrations that combine visual, proprioceptive, and action data. However, collecting such datasets with mobile manipulators is often slowed down by fragmented teleoperation interfaces and manual post-processing workflows. This paper presents an integrated framework for teleoperated multimodal data collection on a Clearpath Dingo-O mobile base equipped with a Kinova Gen3 Lite manipulator and an Intel RealSense RGB-D camera. The framework combines single-controller operation of the base, arm, and recording process with an automated ROS-based pipeline for data capture, image-quality filtering, temporal synchronization, visualization, and export to machine-learning-ready Zarr datasets. We validate the system on a pick-and-place mobile-manipulation task in which the robot transports a bottle between two surfaces. The results show that the unified controller supports complete task execution and recording without external commands, while the data pipeline produces synchronized datasets for short demonstrations and identifies corrupted visual frames. The proposed framework provides a practical basis for producing structured demonstrations for robot-learning research.

Render the Robot, Generate the World: One Video World Model Across Embodiments

Yash Jangir, Karan Mirakhor, Renos Zabounidis, Muhammad Zubair Irshad, Sergey Zakharov, Yonatan Bisk, Homanga Bharadhwaj

Published 2026-11-19 02:52 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO

Takeaway

Doppel targets video world modeling that predicts both body motion and environment response, arguing that geometry and kinematics already specify intended motion better than numerical actions alone. It conditions an embodiment-agnostic video model on camera-aligned renderings of future body configurations plus action trajectories, recovering camera poses to align rendered trajectories with recorded observations, and jointly trains one shared backbone and adapter on eight robot and human embodiments using about 600 hours of video without embodiment labels. Across three robot-arm datasets it reports PSNR gains of 1.00 dB on held-out trajectories and 1.20 dB out-of-distribution over action-only baselines, with Doppel-8 improving over Doppel-3 on four of six evaluation sets. Original abstract

Predicting the consequences of an agent's actions requires modeling both the body's motion and the environment's response. Most video world models attempt to learn both from numerical actions, even though geometry and kinematics already specify the intended motion. We introduce Doppel, an embodiment-agnostic video world model conditioned on camera-aligned renderings of future body configurations and numerical action trajectories. By explicitly providing the intended motion, Doppel can focus on predicting object motion and interaction-driven changes in the environment. Rendered motion also provides a common visual interface across different embodiments and action spaces. To train on robot datasets without camera extrinsics, we recover camera poses and align rendered trajectories with recorded observations. We jointly train a single shared video backbone and conditioning adapter on eight datasets spanning eight robot and human embodiments, from single- and bimanual robot arms to a dexterous humanoid and human hands and bodies, using about 600 hours of video without embodiment labels. Across three robot-arm datasets, Doppel improves PSNR over action-only baselines by 1.00 dB on held-out trajectories and 1.20 dB under out-of-distribution evaluation, while predicting more consistent object motion. Training on all eight embodiments (Doppel-8) improves robot predictions over the three-robot model (Doppel-3) on four of six evaluation sets. Project page: https://doppelwm.github.io/.

Original abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models generate semantically meaningful manipulation actions but often lack sufficient physical-world awareness, making downstream execution vulnerable to task failures caused by physical interaction risks. To address this limitation, we propose a low-cost post-policy correction scheme. The central challenge is to mitigate pose-induced, gripper-local collision risks without aggressive projections that distort the task trajectory encoded by the upstream policy. We formulate VLA execution-risk reduction as a low-intervention, end-effector-local, pose-aware action-space correction problem. Our method leaves both the gripper command and upstream VLA policy unchanged, constructs first-order QP constraints using gripper-local proxy points and the end-effector pose Jacobian, and integrates active obstacle alignment, contact-scope filtering, hand-priority top-$K$selection, correction clipping, and risk-adaptive blending for targeted intervention. Across all 400 official Level I and Level II initial states of SafeLIBERO Spatial, the method increases task success by34.0 percentage pointsand EE-local safe success by27.3 percentage points, while reducing EE-local collision events by47.5%(relative). A 600-trial SO101 feasibility study across two challenge scenes further reduces the visible gripper–obstacle contact rate by30.0 percentage pointscompared with center-point correction. These results demonstrate that pose-aware, gripper-local reasoning achieves a more favorable balance between task progress and local risk reduction without policy retraining or global replanning.
