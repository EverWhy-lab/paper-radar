Learning from Failure: Robot Self-Improvement via Human Videos

Hanzhi Chen, Anran Zhang, Simon Schaefer, Kejia Chen, Shi Chen, Daniel Cremers, Oier Mees, Stefan Leutenegger

Published 2026-06-19 21:17 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO, cs.CV

Takeaway

Failed executions do not reveal which actions would have succeeded, and expert corrections require costly monitoring, so this work introduces Dynamics-Guided Action Correction (DGAC), which uses dynamics and value models pretrained on human videos to construct alternative actions at failed states and evaluate their outcomes without executing each candidate. They also report that human-video dynamics and value models provide corrective supervision for policies pretrained on either human or robot data. Original abstract

Learning from deployment experience is essential for robots to improve their performance in the real world. A robot should be able to learn from failed executions, but these do not reveal which actions would have succeeded, and obtaining corrections from human experts requires costly monitoring and intervention. Human videos offer a rich source of interaction experience, showing how actions change the environment and lead to task completion. To turn this experience into corrective supervision, we introduce Dynamics-Guided Action Correction (DGAC), which uses dynamics and value models pretrained on human videos to construct alternative actions at failed states and evaluate their outcomes without executing each candidate. We evaluate our method on seven real-world manipulation tasks across two robot embodiments and demonstrate that it improves the average success rate from 40% to 81%, requiring no expert corrections during self-improvement. We further show that generated corrections enable recovery from failed executions and provide more effective training targets than imagined policy rollouts using the same models and data. Finally, we demonstrate that human-video dynamics and value models provide effective corrective supervision for policies pretrained on either human or robot data, suggesting that human videos benefit robot learning in addition to policy initialization.

WeaveVLA: 3D Relational Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Models

Zien Wang, Yuhao Pan, Yunfeng Fan, Nan Cheng, Xin Xia, Wenchao Xu

Published 2026-11-24 01:59 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO, cs.CV

Takeaway

WeaveVLA targets the implicit understanding of task-relevant 3D relations and their evolution during manipulation in VLA models, encoding relative 3D relations among objects, the gripper, and nearby references with a task-conditioned sparse graph neural network, maintaining spatial context through pair-specific recurrent edge states, and summarizing the graph into relational tokens that condition the action expert alongside the vision-language context, with an auxiliary geometry reconstruction objective. Instantiated on StarVLA-GR00T and π0.5, it reports over 98% average success on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus gains from 74.9% to 82.0% and from 85.7% to 87.8%, and on three real-world tasks an improvement from 55.6% to 70.0% under standard conditions and from 33.3% to 53.3% under OOD perturbations. Inspect how relational tokens are integrated with the action expert and what the geometry reconstruction objective adds.

Original abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models leverage pretrained vision-language representations for instruction-conditioned robot control, yet their understanding of task-relevant 3D relations and how these relations evolve during manipulation remains largely implicit. To address this challenge, we introduce \textbf{WeaveVLA}, a framework that enhances VLA action generation by explicitly modeling task-relevant relative 3D relations and maintaining relational context across observations. WeaveVLA employs a task-conditioned sparse graph neural network to encode relative 3D relations among objects, the gripper, and nearby references. Pair-specific recurrent edge states integrate geometric evidence across observations to maintain evolving spatial context. A learned-query readout summarizes the updated graph representations into compact relational tokens, which condition the action expert alongside the original vision-language context. An auxiliary geometry reconstruction objective further encourages spatial structure preservation. We instantiate WeaveVLA on StarVLA-GR00T and $π_{0.5}$, achieving over 98\% average success on LIBERO with both backbones. On LIBERO-Plus, WeaveVLA improves success from 74.9\% to 82.0\% and from 85.7\% to 87.8\%, respectively, with additional improvements on CALVIN D$\rightarrow$D. On three real-world tasks, the StarVLA-GR00T-based variant improves average success from 55.6\% to 70.0\% under standard conditions and from 33.3\% to 53.3\% under OOD perturbations, surpassing the $π_{0.5}$ baseline in both settings.

CC-VLA: Learning Control-Aware Compliance VLA Model for Robust Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation

Zhaohong Mai, Chao Wang, Chao Zeng, Sitong Mao, Heng Zhang, Shunbo Zhou, Chenguang Yang

Published 2026-10-01 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters

Takeaway

Force-aware VLA models often show limited precise force tracking and rapid successive adjustment because of action-chunk execution and perception-to-control latency, which can cause excessive interaction forces without timely correction. CC-VLA addresses this with a multimodal mixture-of-experts (MoE) encoding force signal sequences together with vision-language fused features, a multi-stage training strategy for force perception under sparse sampling, a VLA-guided adaptive compliance controller for position tracking in contact-free motion and force-position tracking in contact-rich tasks, and an adversarial shared teleoperation strategy for collecting high-precision force/torque demonstrations. Real-world experiments report improved success rates on challenging force-perception tasks and enhanced force-control precision, with multi-level safety and robustness under tested partial-OOD pose-shift settings. Inspect how the compliance controller interfaces with action-chunk execution and how force signals enter the MoE.

Original abstract

Integrating visuomotor policies or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with force/torque (F/T) perception has demonstrated significant progress in imitation learning for robotic manipulation. However, existing force-aware VLA models frequently exhibit limited capability in precise force tracking and rapid successive adjustments. This deficiency stems from the limitations of action-chunk execution strategies and the substantial latency between perception and real-time control. Such limitations can lead to task failures and safety risks, particularly when the execution of an action chunk exerts excessive interaction forces without timely adjustment. To overcome this challenge, we propose the Control-aware Compliance VLA (CC-VLA) framework for reactive control. The CC-VLA model employs a multimodal mixture-of-experts (MoE) to encode force signal sequences and vision-language fused feature. Furthermore, it utilizes a multi-stage training strategy to ensure robust perception within the visual-semantic space and effective force perception under sparse sampling conditions. Additionally, a VLA-guided adaptive compliance controller is designed to facilitate precise position tracking during contact-free motion and optimal force-position tracking for contact-rich tasks. To facilitate high-precision F/T data acquisition, we also implement an adversaria shared teleoperation strategy for contact-rich demonstrations that bolsters system safety and interactivity. Extensive real-world experiments demonstrate that CC-VLA significantly improves success rates in challenging force-perception tasks and enhances force-control precision, while providing multi-level safety and robustness under the tested partial-OOD pose-shift settings.

Opt2Skill: Imitating Dynamically-Feasible Whole-Body Trajectories for Versatile Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Fukang Liu, Zhaoyuan Gu, Yilin Cai, Ziyi Zhou, Hyunyoung Jung, Jaehwi Jang, Shijie Zhao, Sehoon Ha, Yue Chen, Danfei Xu, Ye Zhao

Published 2025-10-13 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation LettersReading focus sim_to_real, loco_manipulation, trajectory_optimization

Takeaway

Humanoid loco-manipulation is difficult because of high-dimensional unstable dynamics and contact-rich tasks, with model-based optimal control limited by computation and contact sensing and RL limited by inefficient learning, unnatural motion, and sim-to-real gaps. Opt2Skill combines model-based trajectory optimization with RL: differential dynamic programming (DDP) generates dynamically feasible, contact-consistent reference motions for the Digit humanoid, and RL policies are trained to track these trajectories. The authors report that Opt2Skill outperforms baselines using human demonstrations and inverse-kinematics-based references in motion tracking and task success, that trajectories with torque information improve contact force tracking in tasks such as wiping a table, and that the approach was transferred to real-world applications. Inspect how DDP references are made contact-consistent and how torque information is incorporated into tracking.

Original abstract

Humanoid robots are designed to perform diverse loco-manipulation tasks. However, they face challenges due to their high-dimensional and unstable dynamics, as well as the complex contact-rich nature of the tasks. Model-based optimal control methods offer flexibility to define precise motion but are limited by high computational complexity and accurate contact sensing. On the other hand, reinforcement learning (RL) handles high-dimensional spaces with strong robustness but suffers from inefficient learning, unnatural motion, and sim-to-real gaps. To address these challenges, we introduce Opt2Skill, an end-to-end pipeline that combines model-based trajectory optimization with RL to achieve robust whole-body loco-manipulation. Opt2Skill generates dynamic feasible and contact-consistent reference motions for the Digit humanoid robot using differential dynamic programming (DDP) and trains RL policies to track these optimal trajectories. Our results demonstrate that Opt2Skill outperforms baselines that rely on human demonstrations and inverse kinematics-based references, both in motion tracking and task success rates. Furthermore, we show that incorporating trajectories with torque information improves contact force tracking in contact-involved tasks, such as wiping a table. We have successfully transferred our approach to real-world applications.https://opt2skill.github.io

Xing Li, Oliver Brock

Published 2026-11-20 Source The International Journal of Robotics Research

The authors argue that on top of this bias, simple and well-established components suffice for an interactive LfD system that generalizes to unseen variations in object pose, local geometry, and contact conditions under real-world uncertainty. Original abstract

We present a four-stage Learning from Demonstration (LfD) pipeline that incrementally constructs a general policy for multi-stage contact-rich manipulation from a single human demonstration. The pipeline is built around a crucial inductive bias—environmental constraints—that separates general task structure from hard-to-model instance-specific details. We show that, on top of this inductive bias, simple and well-established components are already sufficient to build an interactive LfD system that generalizes to unseen variations in object pose, local geometry, and contact conditions under real-world uncertainty. We validate the system on seven real-world multi-stage contact-rich manipulation tasks and achieve over 90% success across tasks. These results establish environmental constraints as fundamental building blocks for efficient generalization in learning from demonstration.
