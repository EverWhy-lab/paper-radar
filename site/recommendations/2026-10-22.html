Original abstract

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effective interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single action representation for both task intent and whole-body coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms. SP-VLA, a vision-language-action model, first predicts task-relevant sparse poses and finger joint positions from multimodal observations and language instructions using asynchronous action-chunk inference. Given these sparse targets, SP-MGM, a motion generation model trained through two-stage distillation, reconstructs coordinated full-body motion in real time. A pretrained motion encoder maps this motion to a compact latent representation. SP-GMT, a general motion tracker, then integrates the explicit sparse targets with this implicit motion prior for whole-body control. Experiments in simulation and on a real humanoid robot demonstrate improved motion tracking, whole-body coordination, and loco-manipulation success over existing approaches. Additional results and videos are available on our \href{https://sp-hlm.github.io/}{\textcolor{blue}{project page}}.

Watch Your Step: Anticipated Perception for Humanoid Locomotion

Matthias Heyrman, Chenhao Li, Ioannis Dadiotis, Marco Hutter

Published 2026-10-21 19:52 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO, cs.LG

Takeaway

This method targets perceptive humanoid locomotion, where dense robot-centric elevation maps contain information beyond the next footsteps and learned encoders must discover relevance while also learning locomotion. A lightweight foothold prediction model uses proprioception and the velocity command to predict future footholds, and the elevation map is sampled only in small patches around them, with the predictor periodically retrained on new policy rollouts. Original abstract

Perceptive locomotion policies typically observe a dense robot-centric elevation map, although only the terrain beneath and around the next footsteps informs the control decision. Learned encoders compress the whole visual input, leaving the policy to work out which inputs matter while learning the baseline locomotion task. Learning both at once slows training and, without computationally expensive attention mechanisms, makes generalization to new terrain harder. We instead remove redundant inputs from the elevation map before the policy sees it. A lightweight foothold prediction model (FPM) predicts future footholds from proprioception and the velocity command alone, and we sample the elevation map only in small patches around these predicted footholds. We train the prediction model alongside the locomotion policy, periodically retraining it on new policy rollouts as the robot's gait matures. This allows the perception to anticipate where the policy will step, while the policy learns to leverage terrain information around those steps. With the proposed lightweight preprocessing, the policy reaches a given terrain difficulty in roughly a third of the training iterations required with the complete map. It outperforms policies that observe the complete map where footholds are sparse, including in narrow walled corridors, and on unseen terrain, and it matches an attention-based encoder trained at several times the cost. The policy transfers zero-shot to a Unitree G1 humanoid traversing various obstacles, stairs, gaps, and step-downs beyond the training distribution in both indoor and outdoor environments.
