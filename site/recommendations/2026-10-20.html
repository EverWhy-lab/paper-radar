Robot Learning with Vision-Language-Action Models for Agricultural Manipulation

Original abstract

Robotic manipulation in agricultural environments remains challenging due to variations in plant geometry, occlusion, and camera viewpoints. Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) and generalist robot policies offer a promising approach to addressing these challenges by learning manipulation behaviors from multimodal observations and language instructions. However, adapting these policies to agricultural tasks requires domain-specific demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to collect on physical robots. In this work, we develop a simulation-to-real robot-learning framework for agricultural reaching and grasping that integrates task-specific demonstration collection and pretrained policy adaptation. A MuJoCo-based agricultural environment is developed with diverse tomato plant structures to support demonstration collection and controlled evaluation. Expert demonstrations are collected through joystick teleoperation, with a low-level controller used to execute robot actions. Two pretrained generalist robot policies, Octo and $π_{0.5}$, are adapted using simulated demonstrations, followed by further fine-tuning on a physical Franka Research 3 manipulator using real-robot demonstrations. The adapted policies are evaluated under in-distribution, viewpoint/spatial-shift, and out-of-distribution conditions in simulation, followed by physical-robot experiments. The influence of proprioceptive information on policy performance is also investigated. Results show that $π_{0.5}$ achieves higher task-performance scores than Octo and exhibits less degradation under distribution shifts. These findings demonstrate the potential of integrating pretrained generalist policies with task-specific demonstration learning for agricultural manipulation.

Anti-Gravity Walking by a Flying Humanoid Robot via Thrust-Rate Input Whole-Body Model Predictive Control

Kazuki Sugihara, Kei Okada

Published 2026-09-18 Source IEEE Robotics and Automation LettersReading focus model_predictive_control, trajectory_optimization

Takeaway

Flying humanoids have been limited mainly to aerial flight and ground walking, and walking on ceilings or similar anti-gravity surfaces is complicated by thrust spikes when contact switching discontinuously changes the dynamic structure. The authors formulate whole-body MPC with thrust-rate as the control input to keep thrust trajectories continuous across contact switching while preserving the sparse optimal-control structure for fast computation, and they add lower bounds on the foot-normal contact force that are smoothly transferred during double support. They report implementing the framework and demonstrating anti-gravity walking by a flying humanoid in simulation and a hardware experiment, described as a first demonstration of multi-contact whole-body MPC for a transformable aerial robot and of walking beyond the ground. Inspect the contact-switching formulation and force-bound transfer.

Original abstract

Flying humanoids are expected to perform tasks in diverse environments, while their existing locomotion is mainly limited to aerial flight and ground walking. The capability to move in complex three-dimensional space can greatly expand their application range. For such walking motion on ceilings and similar anti-gravity environments, whole-body MPC is effective. However, the discontinuous changes in dynamic structure accompanying contact switching during walking can induce thrust spikes, resulting in control instability. Therefore, in this work, we propose and implement a real-time whole-body MPC framework for anti-gravity bipedal walking. First, we formulate whole-body MPC using the time derivative of thrust, namely thrust-rate, as the control input. This formulation guarantees continuity of the thrust trajectory during contact switching while preserving the sparse structure of the optimal control problem for fast computation. Second, we address the lack of natural support forces in anti-gravity environments. We introduce lower bounds on the foot-normal component of the contact force, and smoothly transfer them during the doublesupport phase. Finally, we implement the proposed framework and demonstrate anti-gravity walking by a flying humanoid through simulation and a hardware experiment. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first demonstration of multi-contact whole-body MPC for a transformable aerial robot and walking by a flying humanoid beyond the ground.

Original abstract

General-purpose manipulation requires reliable behavior across embodiments, objects, and task demands. We introduce HandsOn, a composable simulation platform and benchmark for cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation. It supports five robotic arms, six dexterous hands, and approximately 200 rigid, articulated, and deformable assets in randomized scenes. The benchmark pairs 18,000 curated human demonstrations with 66 human-verified tasks: Core-50 supports demonstration-based learning and execution tests, while Transfer-16 evaluates asset and relation transfer, skill composition, and deformable manipulation without target-task training demonstrations. Qualitative real-robot rollouts illustrate sim-to-real transfer. Across diffusion, vision-language-action, world-action, and prompted VLM-based methods, the best trained multi-task policy achieves 29.1% Core-50 success. Without benchmark finetuning, GPT-6 using move_eef for palm-and-finger control achieves 5.2% on Core-50 and 1.9% on Transfer-16 over 660 trials. Trained-policy stage diagnostics expose failures in contact establishment, action coordination, and maintaining achieved goals, with additional sequencing errors in composed tasks. HandsOn provides a shared basis for investigating manipulation generalization and the limits of reliable physical interaction.
