EqC: Equilibrium Energy Control for Introspective Lifelong Robot Learning

Original abstract

Long-term robot autonomy depends on more than effective action generation: a policy must recognize when its behavior is no longer sufficiently supported and use assistance to improve. We introduce Equilibrium Energy Control (EqC), a framework for introspective lifelong robot learning built around an explicit conditional energy landscape. EqC casts generative action refinement as equilibrium-seeking optimization, while the geometry of the refinement process reveals whether the generated behavior remains consistent with the learned landscape. This competence signal enables Energy-Gated DAgger, where persistent energy-consistency violations trigger expert takeover and the resulting recovery trajectories are aggregated for subsequent policy updates. EqC integrates naturally into both Diffusion Transformer policies and flow-matching-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies. Across simulation and real-world bimanual manipulation, EqC matches or improves control performance and robustness while enabling timely intervention near the policy's competence boundary. A real-world study further shows how these interventions progressively expand the range of situations handled autonomously, providing a practical path from static generative controllers toward self-improving robot systems.

Contact Trajectory Prompting: In-Context Transfer of Contact-Rich Behaviors from a Single Demonstration

Xian Nie, Yujie Zang, Yuhang Zheng, Yupeng Zheng, Songen Gu, Wendi Chen, Chuan Wen, Cewu Lu, Wenchao Ding, Junchi Yan, Shuicheng Yan

Original abstract

In-context robot learning enables robots to acquire new behaviors from demonstrations without test-time parameter updates. However, visual and kinematic specifications alone may not distinguish contact-rich behaviors that share similar motions but require different interaction intensities or patterns. We propose Contact Trajectory Prompting (CTP), a framework for one-shot transfer of contact-rich manipulation behaviors. CTP encodes motion and contact information from a single demonstration into an ordered contact trajectory prompt that captures their temporal evolution and specifies the desired physical interaction behavior. During execution, the policy uses online sensory feedback to retrieve interaction-relevant information from the prompt, conditioning closed-loop action generation on the demonstrated contact behavior. Across real-world wiping, rotation, and insertion tasks on objects excluded from training, CTP achieves 78.7% overall success, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline by 13.0 percentage points. Changing only the reference demonstration enables CTP to follow contact specifications for both intensity and direction, including an intermediate wiping force level and clockwise or counterclockwise cap rotation. These results demonstrate that contact trajectories serve as effective behavioral specifications, extending in-context robot learning beyond motion imitation to physical interaction transfer. Original abstract

Robotic contact-rich and fine-grained manipulation remains a significant challenge due to complex interaction dynamics and the competing requirements of multi-timescale control. While current visual imitation learning methods excel at long-horizon planning, they often fail to perceive critical interaction cues like friction variations or incipient slip, and struggle to balance global task coherence with local reactive feedback. To address these challenges, we propose M2-ResiPolicy, a novel Master-Micro residual control architecture that synergizes high-level action guidance with low-level correction. The framework consists of a Master-Guidance Policy (MGP) operating at 10 Hz, which generates temporally consistent action chunks via a diffusion-based backbone and employs a tactile-intensity-driven adaptive fusion mechanism to dynamically modulate perceptual weights between vision and touch. Simultaneously, a high-frequency (60 Hz) Micro-Residual Corrector (MRC) utilizes a lightweight GRU to provide real-time action compensation based on TCP wrench feedback. This policy is further integrated with a force-mixed PBIC execution layer, effectively regulating contact forces to ensure interaction safety. Experiments across several demanding tasks including fragile object grasping and precision insertion, demonstrate that M2-ResiPolicy significantly outperforms standard Diffusion Policy (DP) and state-of-the-art Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP), achieving a 93\% damage-free success rate in chip grasping and superior force regulation stability.

Model-Based Online Decision Making via Generative Trajectory Planning

Haldun Balim, Yilun Du, Na Li

Original abstract

Online decision-making faces a tension between efficient inference and long-horizon reasoning. Value-based methods rely on short-horizon bootstrapping that limits the propagation of long-term information, while trajectory optimization methods plan explicitly but must solve a high-dimensional optimization problem at every decision step. We propose Generative Trajectory Planning (GTP), a model-based framework that addresses this tension by learning a generative model as a proposal distribution over action sequences. Instead of optimizing sequences from scratch, GTP samples candidate sequences from a diffusion prior and refines them using learned dynamics, reward, and value models, enabling efficient trajectory-level reasoning. Across continuous control and long-horizon manipulation tasks, GTP matches or exceeds state-of-the-art model-free, diffusion-policy, and model-based baselines, and remains stable in regimes where strong baselines degrade. Our results suggest that generative models are best understood not as policies, but as structured proposal distributions for planning, offering a new perspective on how to integrate generative modeling with model-based decision-making.

Dexterous Manipulation Through Imitation Learning: A Survey

Takeaway

This survey reviews dexterous manipulation through imitation learning, covering robotic hands or multi-fingered end-effectors that control, reorient, and manipulate objects via coordinated finger movements and adaptive force modulation. It frames model-based approaches as struggling to generalize given high dimensionality and complex contact dynamics, and model-free reinforcement learning as demanding extensive training, large-scale interaction data, and carefully designed rewards, positioning imitation learning (IL) as acquiring skills from expert demonstrations while bypassing explicit modeling and large-scale trial-and-error. The survey details recent advances, addresses key challenges, and explores potential research directions for IL-driven dexterous manipulation, aiming to give researchers and practitioners a comprehensive introduction. Original abstract

Dexterous manipulation, which refers to the ability of a robotic hand or multi-fingered end-effector to skillfully control, reorient, and manipulate objects through precise, coordinated finger movements and adaptive force modulation, enables complex interactions similar to human hand dexterity. With recent advances in robotics and machine learning, there is a growing demand for these systems to operate in complex and unstructured environments. Traditional model-based approaches struggle to generalize across tasks and object variations due to the high dimensionality and complex contact dynamics of dexterous manipulation. Although model-free methods such as reinforcement learning (RL) show promise, they require extensive training, large-scale interaction data, and carefully designed rewards for stability and effectiveness. Imitation learning (IL) offers an alternative by allowing robots to acquire dexterous manipulation skills directly from expert demonstrations, capturing fine-grained coordination and contact dynamics while bypassing the need for explicit modeling and large-scale trial-and-error. This survey provides an overview of dexterous manipulation methods based on imitation learning, details recent advances, and addresses key challenges in the field. Additionally, it explores potential research directions to enhance IL-driven dexterous manipulation. Our goal is to offer researchers and practitioners a comprehensive introduction to this rapidly evolving domain.
