TacClaw-Infinity: A Large-Scale Vision-Tactile-Language-Action Dataset for Dexterous Manipulation

Original abstract

Large-scale robot learning increasingly relies on diverse human demonstrations, yet tactile manipulation data remain difficult to scale, and existing multimodal datasets often lack consistent quality control and structured supervision. We present TacClaw-Infinity, a large-scale structured Vision-Tactile-Language-Action (VTLA) dataset for contact-rich manipulation, collected with TacClaw, a tactile-enabled UMI-style handheld interface that records synchronized visual observations, fingertip tactile signals, and end-effector trajectories without requiring a target robot during data collection. TacClaw-Infinity is built through a distributed worldwide collection across homes, laboratories, industrial workplaces, retail stores, and outdoor environments including tasks contributed by academic and industrial collaborators. Together, these efforts yield over 10,000 hours, 1 million episodes, and 1 billion frames spanning more than 1,000 task types. We develop a scalable pipeline for trajectory reconstruction, multimodal quality control, embodiment-aware filtering, and structured annotation, covering contact-centric segmentation, subtask description, and spatial grounding. We validate TacClaw-Infinity at both the data and model levels: At the data level, we assess the collection interface, the action-space compatibility of the curated demonstrations, and the structured annotations, while for the model level, we demonstrate cross-embodiment transfer across three heterogeneous robots without robot-specific demonstrations.

Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation

Xiaoman Li, Zhiqiang Yang, Jiayu Hu, Jipeng Kong, Yang Zhang, Jingwei Zhang, Fangzheng Yan, Chunhua Shen, Chenjia Bai

Original abstract

Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. More details and videos are available at: https://gr-bc.github.io

Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields

Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz

Published 2026-10-14 22:25 Asia/Shanghai Categories cs.RO Reading focus safety_critical_control

Takeaway

This work addresses safety failures in robot reinforcement learning that emerge over several steps rather than instantaneously, as policies enter unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. It learns a receding-horizon safety critic predicting whether a state remains non-terminating over the next horizon, trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and aggregates an ensemble with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate. In simulated quadruped locomotion, the method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns. Original abstract

Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

Original abstract

Compositionality, by organizing complex behavior as combinations of simpler elements, enables robot learning that is scalable and data efficient. Leveraging this principle, we propose a generative learning-from-demonstration framework that enables compositional modeling of robotic behavior by connecting perception and motion through shared object-level representations. We render scenes from object-centric neural representations that integrate canonical neural fields with latent-conditioned deformations, capturing positional and geometric variations in a smooth, consistent, and interpretable way. For motion generation, a temporal mixture-of-experts (MoE) employs a gating mechanism to combine object-conditioned movement primitives over time, producing complete trajectories. This spatial-temporal compositionality maintains the data efficiency of movement primitives while grounding motion in visual structure, enabling systematic generalization across diverse scene configurations. In simulation, long-horizon manipulation tasks are successfully completed using the proposed model, which requires significantly less training data than other image-based baselines. Real-world experiments further demonstrate the method's robustness to noise, its ability to generalize at the category level through language-based segmentation models, and its capacity to operate directly on 3D scene representations.
