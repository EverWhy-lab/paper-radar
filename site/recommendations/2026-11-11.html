LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning

Original abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

Original abstract

We address key challenges in Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) for real-world contact-rich manipulation: how to collect informative human correction data and how to effectively update policies with this new data. We introduce Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger), which contains two novel components: (1) a Compliant Intervention Interface that leverages compliance control, allowing humans to provide gentle, accurate delta action corrections without interrupting the ongoing robot policy execution; and (2) a Compliant Residual Policy formulation that learns from human corrections while incorporating force feedback and force control. Our system significantly enhances performance on precise contact-rich manipulation tasks using minimal correction data, improving base policy success rates by 64% on four challenging tasks (book flipping, belt assembly, cable routing, and gear insertion) while outperforming both retraining-from-scratch and finetuning approaches. Through extensive real-world experiments, we provide practical guidance for implementing effective DAgger in real-world robot learning tasks. Result videos are available at: https://compliant-residual-dagger.github.io/ .

The method separates the reference observed by the actor from the target used to train execution, using a rule-generated edited reference to guide tracking rewards during proximal policy optimization while deployed observation and action interfaces stay unchanged. Original abstract

Humanoid motion interfaces should accommodate application-specific movement constraints while retaining useful reference behavior. Local content redaction requires a controller to replace selected movements even when it continues to receive the original reference. Removing the tracking incentive for those movements leaves their replacement unspecified. We introduce RedirectMimic, which separates the reference observed by the actor from the target used to train its execution. A rule-generated edited reference guides tracking rewards during proximal policy optimization, while the deployed observation and action interfaces remain unchanged. We evaluate pose- and speed-defined arm editing in Unitree G1 simulation using a tracker pretrained on 7,509 motion clips. On the overhead-arm task, RedirectMimic reduces core-window content occurrence to 2.7%, compared with 58.9% for reward zeroing and 32.8% for anti-tracking. It achieves 94.2% core-window joint success, combining content suppression, fall-free execution, and local retention. On the speed-defined task, fast outward arm movements occurred at similarly low rates under all three local editing methods. Across both tasks, RedirectMimic achieved the lowest replacement error among these methods and tracked unedited motions with accuracy comparable to that of the pretrained controller. Project page: https://xiaopeng-whu.github.io/RedirectMimic/.

Original abstract

For humanoid robots to transition from isolated environments to shared workspaces, safety is a key requirement. While technical safety standards for humanoid robots are currently under development, the perspectives of the workers who will collaborate with these machines remain limited. This paper studies worker preferences for humanoid safety by conducting an experiment with 1,096 U.S. frontline workers in manufacturing and logistics, followed by in-depth qualitative interviews with a subset of 24 workers. By presenting participants with randomized hypothetical job profiles, we quantify the trade-offs workers make between various safety attributes. We find the following key results. First, U.S. workers are willing to give up as much as 5% of their hourly wage to work with humanoids that have clear communication intent. Second, despite potential data privacy concerns, workers are willing to give up as much as 4% of their hourly wage to work in conditions where data collected by humanoids are used to enhance workplace safety. Third, due to psychological discomfort with unconventional humanoid morphology, workers desire a 2% wage premium to work alongside a centaur humanoid despite its relatively stable, low center of gravity. Finally, exploratory analysis indicates that prior workplace exposure to robots reduces preference for an active-steering e-stop among experienced users. These findings offer critical empirical data for roboticists, industry leaders, and policymakers to develop humanoids that are technically safe and take into account workers' perspectives, contributing to the successful integration of robots into the workplace.
